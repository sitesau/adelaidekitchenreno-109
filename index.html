Kitchen Renovation Adelaide What to Expect Explained

Based on the parent planning guide, the process opens with a no obligation quote step: you describe the job, talk through scope with vetted Adelaide renovators, and appoint the one you trust. Knowing the likely order of events helps you prepare the right questions at each stage. The broad stages most projects move through are:

  • Brief and scope: decide whether the footprint stays and identify the project type, whether a facelift, small kitchen update, full remodel, butler's pantry or open plan conversion.
  • Design and drawings: lock layouts, cabinetry runs, appliance positions and finishes before manufacture is booked.
  • Ordering and templating: cabinets go to the workshop, then stonemasons template, fabricate and install stone benchtops once cabinet and appliance dimensions are settled.
  • Trades in sequence: electricians, plumbers, tilers and stonemasons each arrive in a planned order, which is why unresolved decisions cause delays.
  • Install and handover: appliances are fitted, finishes checked, and variations reconciled against the written scope.

Who this page suits

This angle suits Adelaide homeowners who have accepted the kitchen needs work but want to understand the experience before committing to a brief. It is most relevant if you are weighing a new kitchen, a layout redesign, custom joinery, a modest upgrade or a larger open plan change.

  • Footprint kept: ask whether doors, handles, benchtops, splashbacks and appliance fit are fixed items or allowance based.
  • Footprint changed: ask who sequences the electrician, plumber, tiler and stonemason, and what happens when one trade slips.
  • Older homes: the source identifies heritage stone cottages, character homes and Adelaide Hills homes, so ask what similar projects the renovator has finished.
  • Butler's pantry: check storage, circulation, finishes and services in the same design brief as the main kitchen, not as a bolt on.
  • Compact rooms: tighter spaces demand clear appliance positions, landing space and safer workflow.

Reading the cost signals before quotes

The wide published price band exists because scope changes everything. A facelift that keeps the existing footprint is a fundamentally different job to a remodel spanning kitchen design, custom cabinetry, stone benchtops, splashbacks, appliances and multiple trades. Give every renovator identical inputs so totals can be compared fairly:

  • Room photos and rough measurements
  • Layout changes and appliance changes
  • Preferred benchtop material and splashback type
  • Storage needs and a realistic budget range

Then ask each quote to separate cabinetry, joinery, benchtops, splashbacks, appliances, electrical work, plumbing work, tiling, installation and coordination. Design decisions that become trade decisions

Once cabinets are ordered, design choices turn into manufacturing and scheduling commitments. An open plan kitchen can alter circulation, lighting and the position of services. A butler's pantry only performs when its storage, access and finishes are resolved alongside the main kitchen. For custom cabinetry, review drawers, pantry runs and corner units before signing off drawings; the source names soft close drawers, bespoke pull outs and full height pantry runs, so confirm whether each is included, optional or excluded. With stone benchtops and splashbacks, establish when measuring, templating and installation occur, because stonemasons template, fabricate and install in sequence. Any unresolved appliance or cabinet dimension should be final before that stage begins.

Approvals, credentials and local fit

The source states that approvals, where needed, follow PlanSA approval guidance and the Planning and Design Code. It does not claim every kitchen needs approval, so ask whether your scope triggers one, particularly where layout, structure or heritage considerations are involved. Relevant credentials to verify include Housing Industry Association standards, Master Builders South Australia accreditation for many builders, and Consumer and Business Services licensing checks. Before appointing anyone, request contractor licence details, supervisor registration where relevant, and a written scope naming who handles design, manufacture, installation and trade coordination. Local fit is proven through house type: ask what changed in past quotes because the home was a heritage cottage, an Adelaide Hills character home or a modern open plan family layout.

The one page brief that sharpens every quote

Prepare a single page before prices are discussed. Record what fails in the current kitchen, what must stay, what can move, appliance changes, preferred cabinetry level, benchtop preference, splashback preference, storage priorities and budget range. Then put the same questions to each renovator: who designs the layout, who builds the cabinets, who measures for stone, who schedules the trades, what is excluded, which decisions must be final before manufacture, and how variations are priced after acceptance. Compare inclusions before comparing totals. A lower figure should still identify coordination responsibility; a higher one should explain the extra design, joinery, finish or management it covers.

  1. Define the project honestly. State whether the job is a facelift, small upgrade, full remodel, butler's pantry or open plan conversion, and decide if the footprint stays.
  2. Prepare identical brief inputs. Give every renovator the same photos, measurements, layout and appliance changes, and preferred materials so quotes can be compared.
  3. Verify credentials and approvals. Check contractor licence details, supervisor registration where relevant, HIA standards, Master Builders SA accreditation and whether your scope raises a PlanSA approval issue.
  4. Approve decisions before manufacture. Settle cabinet and appliance dimensions before stonemasons measure, template and install benchtops.
  5. Reconcile variations against scope. Track exclusions, decision deadlines and how variations are priced after acceptance, using the written scope as the reference.
